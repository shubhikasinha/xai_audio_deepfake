\begin{abstract}
Audio deepfake detectors achieve strong benchmark accuracy, yet post-hoc explanations---attribution maps that analysts rely on to understand why a sample is flagged---have never been systematically stress-tested under real-world transmission degradations. This paper presents the first study explicitly decoupling detection accuracy from explanation faithfulness under controlled codec compression and additive noise. Evaluating the AASIST graph-attention detector using Integrated Gradients on evaluation partitions derived from ASVspoof 2021 DF across five degradation conditions, a novel composite metric termed the Explanation Consistency Score (ECS) is introduced, combining attribution stability, spectral band alignment, and faithfulness preservation. Empirical findings reveal a qualitative degradation boundary: Opus 6\,kbps compression induces severe explanation collapse ($\text{ECS} = 0.290 \pm 0.028$, attribution stability falling to $0.184$), whereas moderate bitrate compression (Opus 16\,kbps) and additive noise (AWGN 10--20\,dB) maintain trustworthy explanations ($\text{ECS} > 0.850$). The proposed ECS metric functions as an operational early-warning signal for forensic deployment, proactively flagging untrustworthy explanation states that standard detection metrics alone fail to distinguish.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

The proliferation of synthetic speech---generated by text-to-speech systems,
voice conversion models, and neural vocoders---poses an escalating forensic
challenge.
Audio deepfake detectors based on deep learning have reached impressive accuracy
on standard benchmarks such as ASVspoof~\cite{yamagishi2021asvspoof}, but the
field increasingly recognises that raw detection performance alone is insufficient
for trustworthy deployment.
Forensic analysts, legal practitioners, and platform moderators require not only
a binary verdict but an intelligible explanation of \emph{what spectro-temporal
features} drove the decision~\cite{ge2022explaining}.

Post-hoc explainability methods---principally Integrated Gradients
(\ig{})~\cite{sundararajan2017axiomatic} and Kernel
\shap{}~\cite{lundberg2017unified}---produce saliency maps over
mel-spectrogram representations.
However, a fundamental question remains unasked: \emph{do these explanations
remain faithful and consistent when the audio undergoes the real-world
degradations---codec compression, additive noise, transmission artefacts---that
already challenge detection accuracy?}

This discrepancy creates a \textbf{trust gap}: a detector may sustain its spoof
verdict under moderate degradation, but its explanation may silently shift to
highlight irrelevant spectral regions, rendering the rationale meaningless---or
actively misleading---to an analyst.

\subsection*{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Decoupled Analysis}: A framework decoupling detection accuracy from explanation faithfulness under controlled real-world degradation on ASVspoof 2021 DF across five codec and noise conditions.

  \item \textbf{Explanation Consistency Score (\ecs{})}: A composite metric combining attribution stability (cosine similarity across conditions), spectral band alignment, and faithfulness preservation via Deletion AUC change.

  \item \textbf{Empirical Characterisation of Explanation Collapse}: Demonstration that Opus~6\,kbps compression causes severe explanation collapse ($\text{ECS} = 0.290 \pm 0.028$, stability $= 0.184$) while lighter degradations (Opus~16\,kbps, AWGN at 10--20\,dB SNR) preserve explanation trustworthiness ($\text{ECS} > 0.850$).

  \item \textbf{Forensic Early-Warning Dashboard}: A prototype visualisation mapping conditions to TRUSTED/UNTRUSTED zones, enabling analysts to dynamically adjust reliance on XAI output.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work \& Comparative Positioning}
\label{sec:related}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Audio Deepfake Detection \& Robustness}

Audio deepfake detection has advanced from handcrafted features (LFCC+GMM baselines)
to end-to-end deep architectures such as RawNet2~\cite{tak2021rawnet2} and \aasist{}~\cite{jung2022aasist}, as well as self-supervised speech representations like WavLM~\cite{chen2022wavlm} and wav2vec 2.0~\cite{baevski2020wav2vec}.
\aasist{} combines raw waveform processing with a heterogeneous graph attention network (HGAT), achieving state-of-the-art equal error rates on ASVspoof 2019/2021.
Robustness under distribution shift has been examined by
M\"uller et al.~\cite{muller2022does,muller2024harder} and Valenti et al.~\cite{valenti2024codec}, who show that cross-dataset generalisation and lossy transcoding (e.g., MP3, Opus, AAC) present major vulnerabilities.
However, existing benchmarks evaluate prediction accuracy alone without auditing explanation integrity under shift.

\subsection{Post-Hoc XAI in Speech \& Prior Research}

Ge et al.~\cite{ge2022explaining} applied \shap{} to countermeasures for speaker verification spoofing, revealing which spectral bands drive detection on clean benchmark audio.
Grinberg et al.~\cite{grinberg2025xai} extended attribution to time-domain models.
Shortcut-learning analyses~\cite{yi2022shortcut} raised concerns that detectors
may rely on training-data-specific artefacts rather than genuine spoofing cues.
General XAI faithfulness literature~\cite{samek2016evaluating,petsiuk2018rise,hooker2019benchmark} defines Deletion and Insertion metrics, while robustness studies~\cite{adebayo2018sanity,alvarez2018robustness,dombrowski2019explanations,rosenberg2024evaluation} note that attribution maps can be unstable.
Our work operationalises these concerns into a composite metric: does faithfulness
hold under the same distributional shifts that challenge detection?

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Detector: \aasist{}}

The \aasist{} model~\cite{jung2022aasist} serves as the primary detector.
\aasist{} applies sinc-convolutions on raw waveforms followed by a heterogeneous
graph attention network modelling spectral and temporal dependencies.
The officially released pretrained checkpoint (trained on ASVspoof 2019 LA) is used.

\subsection{Degradation Pipeline}

Five degradation conditions are evaluated (Table~\ref{tab:conditions}).
Codec conditions are applied via \texttt{ffmpeg} with the \texttt{libopus} encoder;
noise conditions inject Gaussian white noise at the specified SNR.
All degradations are deterministic with random seed fixed to 42.

\subsection{Explanation Method: Integrated Gradients}

Integrated Gradients~\cite{sundararajan2017axiomatic} computes attributions by
integrating gradients along the path from baseline $\mathbf{x}'$ (zero waveform)
to input $\mathbf{x}$:
\begin{equation}
  \phi_i(\mathbf{x}) = (x_i - x'_i)\!\int_{\alpha=0}^{1}
    \frac{\partial F(\mathbf{x}' + \alpha(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}'))}{\partial x_i}
    \,d\alpha
\end{equation}
where $F(\cdot)$ is the detector logit.
Interpolation uses $T=20$ steps.
Waveform attributions $\phi(\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{L}$ are projected to the
mel-spectrogram domain via a pre-computed filterbank, yielding a 2-D attribution
map $A\in\mathbb{R}^{F\times T}$ with $F=64$ mel bands.

\subsection{Explanation Consistency Score (ECS)}
\label{subsec:ecs}

\ecs{} is a composite metric quantifying explanation trustworthiness under
degradation condition $d$:
\begin{equation}
  \ecs(x,d) = \alpha\cdot\mathrm{ES}(x,d)
             + \beta\cdot\mathrm{SBA}(d)
             + \gamma\cdot\mathrm{FP}(x,d)
  \label{eq:ecs}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha{+}\beta{+}\gamma{=}1$ ($\alpha{=}0.40,\,\beta{=}0.30,\,\gamma{=}0.30$).

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{Explanation Stability (ES)} measures cosine similarity between clean and
degraded attribution maps:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{ES}(x,d) = \frac{A_{\text{clean}}\cdot A_{d}}%
                          {\|A_{\text{clean}}\|\,\|A_{d}\|}
\end{equation}

\noindent
\textbf{Spectral Band Alignment (SBA)} measures the Pearson correlation across the evaluation sample set $i = 1, \ldots, N$ between attribution energy mass in known vocoder artefact bands $\mathcal{B} = [4.0, 8.0]\text{ kHz}$~\cite{wang2023investigating} and prediction confidence:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{SBA}(d) = \mathrm{Pearson\_r}\!\left(
    \left\{ \sum_{f\in\mathcal{B}} A_{d}^{(i)}[f,:] \right\}_{i=1}^N,\;
    \left\{ P(\text{spoof}\mid x_i,d) \right\}_{i=1}^N \right)
\end{equation}

\noindent
\textbf{Faithfulness Preservation (FP)} measures stability of the Deletion AUC across conditions:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{FP}(x,d) = 1 - \left|\mathrm{DelAUC}_{\text{clean}} -
                            \mathrm{DelAUC}_{d}\right|
\end{equation}

Deployments define $\ecs\geq0.50$ as TRUSTED and $\ecs<0.50$ as UNTRUSTED.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

Evaluation uses $N=100$ utterances derived from the ASVspoof 2021 DF evaluation dataset partition (16\,kHz, 4\,s duration per file), comprising 50 bonafide speech samples and 50 spoofed samples generated across logical access and deepfake attack algorithms A07--A19.
All experimental code and execution parameters are fully documented for complete reproducibility.\footnote{Anonymized code repository, model checkpoints, and experimental logs will be released publicly upon publication.}

\noindent\textbf{Hyperparameters.}
\ig{} steps $T=20$; mel bands $F=64$; hop length $H=512$;
Deletion/Insertion AUC steps $K=10$; \ecs{} weights $\alpha=0.40,\beta=0.30,\gamma=0.30$;
random seed 42.

\noindent\textbf{Statistical analysis.}
Bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals (1{,}000 resamples);
paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests vs. C0 with Bonferroni correction
($\alpha_{\text{corr}}=0.0125$);
Cohen's $d$ for effect sizes.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{The Trust Gap is Real and Condition-Dependent}

Results confirm the hypothesised trust gap.
Under moderate degradation (C8, N1, N2), detection and explanation degrade
smoothly with no dangerous decoupling.
The critical finding is the existence of a \emph{degradation threshold} beyond
which explanation collapses while the detector merely becomes uncertain.

\subsection{Implications for Forensic Deployment}

Three practical guidelines emerge:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Do not rely on XAI explanations for Opus~6\,kbps audio}
    without re-encoding or complementary analysis.
  \item \textbf{AWGN-degraded audio preserves explanation quality better
    than equivalent-severity codec compression}; practitioners can trust
    \aasist{} explanations at 10\,dB SNR.
  \item \textbf{Compute \ecs{} as a pre-analysis step} before presenting
    any XAI-based evidence, flagging untrustworthy conditions for additional review.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations}

Several limitations should be noted.
First, this evaluation focuses on \aasist{} as the primary detector architecture; future work will extend testing to SSL transformer models (WavLM, wav2vec 2.0).
Second, degradation conditions evaluate discrete codecs and stationary Gaussian noise; dynamic multi-hop transcoding and neural codecs (SoundStream, EnCodec) warrant further study.
Third, the zero-waveform baseline used in Integrated Gradients is compared against Gaussian noise baselines, which may influence absolute attribution scores.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

This paper introduced the Explanation Consistency Score (\ecs{}), a composite metric
quantifying whether post-hoc XAI attributions in audio deepfake detectors remain
faithful under real-world degradation.
Evaluating \aasist{} with Integrated Gradients across five conditions derived from ASVspoof 2021 DF, results demonstrated
that \ecs{} functions as a practical early-warning signal for forensic deployment,
flagging untrustworthy explanation states before deployment failure.
