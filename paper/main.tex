\begin{abstract}
Audio deepfake detectors achieve strong benchmark accuracy, yet their post-hoc
explanations---attribution maps that analysts rely on to understand \emph{why} a
sample is flagged---have never been systematically stress-tested under the
real-world degradations that affect deployment.
We present the first study that explicitly \emph{decouples} detection accuracy
from explanation faithfulness under controlled codec compression and additive noise.
Evaluating the \aasist{} graph-attention detector using Integrated Gradients on
five degradation conditions derived from ASVspoof 2021 DF, we introduce the \textbf{Explanation Consistency
Score} (\ecs{}), a novel composite metric combining attribution stability,
spectral band alignment, and faithfulness preservation.
Our central finding is striking: Opus~6\,kbps compression causes complete
explanation collapse (\ecs{}$\approx$0.30, stability$=0$) while higher-bitrate
and noise conditions (\ecs{}$>$0.83) remain trustworthy.
We demonstrate that \ecs{} functions as a practical early-warning signal for forensic deployment,
flagging untrustworthy conditions that traditional detection metrics alone cannot distinguish.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

The proliferation of synthetic speech---generated by text-to-speech systems,
voice conversion models, and neural vocoders---poses an escalating forensic
challenge.
Audio deepfake detectors based on deep learning have reached impressive accuracy
on standard benchmarks such as ASVspoof~\cite{yamagishi2021asvspoof}, but the
field increasingly recognises that raw detection performance alone is insufficient
for trustworthy deployment.
Forensic analysts, legal practitioners, and platform moderators require not only
a binary verdict but an intelligible explanation of \emph{what spectro-temporal
features} drove the decision~\cite{ge2022explaining}.

Post-hoc explainability methods---principally Integrated Gradients
(\ig{})~\cite{sundararajan2017axiomatic} and Kernel
\shap{}~\cite{lundberg2017unified}---produce saliency maps over
mel-spectrogram representations.
However, a fundamental question remains unasked: \emph{do these explanations
remain faithful and consistent when the audio undergoes the real-world
degradations---codec compression, additive noise, transmission artefacts---that
already challenge detection accuracy?}

We identify this as a \textbf{trust gap}: a detector may sustain its spoof
verdict under moderate degradation, but its explanation may silently shift to
highlight irrelevant spectral regions, rendering the rationale meaningless---or
actively misleading---to an analyst.

\subsection*{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{First decoupled analysis} of detection accuracy versus explanation
    faithfulness under controlled real-world degradation on ASVspoof 2021 DF,
    across five codec and noise conditions.

  \item \textbf{Explanation Consistency Score (\ecs{})}: a novel composite metric
    combining attribution stability (cosine similarity across conditions), spectral
    band alignment, and faithfulness preservation via Deletion AUC change, providing
    a single deployable trustworthiness signal.

  \item \textbf{Empirical finding on explanation collapse}: Opus~6\,kbps
    compression causes catastrophic explanation collapse (\ecs{}$\approx$0.30,
    stability$\approx$0) while lighter degradations (Opus~16\,kbps, AWGN at
    10--20\,dB SNR) preserve explanation trustworthiness (\ecs{}$>$0.83).

  \item \textbf{Forensic early-warning dashboard}: a prototype visualisation
    mapping conditions to TRUSTED/UNTRUSTED zones, enabling analysts to
    dynamically adjust reliance on XAI output.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work \& Comparative Positioning}
\label{sec:related}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Audio Deepfake Detection \& Robustness}

Audio deepfake detection has advanced from handcrafted features (LFCC+GMM baselines)
to end-to-end deep architectures.
\aasist{}~\cite{jung2022aasist} combines raw waveform processing with a
heterogeneous graph attention network (HGAT), achieving state-of-the-art equal
error rates on ASVspoof 2019/2021.
Robustness under distribution shift has been examined by
M\"uller et al.~\cite{muller2022does,muller2024harder}, who show that
cross-dataset generalisation remains a central challenge.
However, existing benchmarks evaluate prediction accuracy alone without auditing explanation integrity under shift.

\subsection{Post-Hoc XAI in Speech & Prior AIST Research}

Ge et al.~\cite{ge2022explaining} applied \shap{} to countermeasures for speaker verification spoofing, revealing which spectral bands drive detection on clean benchmark audio.
Grinberg et al.~\cite{grinberg2025xai} extended attribution to time-domain models.
Shortcut-learning analyses~\cite{yi2022shortcut} raised concerns that detectors
may rely on training-data-specific artefacts.
Our work operationalises these concerns into a concrete composite metric: does faithfulness
hold under the same distributional shifts that challenge detection?

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Detector: \aasist{}}

We use the \aasist{} model~\cite{jung2022aasist} as our primary detector.
\aasist{} applies sinc-convolutions on raw waveforms followed by a heterogeneous
graph attention network modelling spectral and temporal dependencies.
We use the officially released pretrained checkpoint (ASVspoof 2019 LA training).

\subsection{Degradation Pipeline}

We apply five degradation conditions (Table~\ref{tab:conditions}).
Codec conditions are applied via \texttt{ffmpeg} with the \texttt{libopus} encoder;
noise conditions inject Gaussian white noise at the specified SNR.
All degradations are deterministic (seed 42).

\subsection{Explanation Method: Integrated Gradients}

Integrated Gradients~\cite{sundararajan2017axiomatic} computes attributions by
integrating gradients along the path from baseline $\mathbf{x}'$ (zero waveform)
to input $\mathbf{x}$:
\begin{equation}
  \phi_i(\mathbf{x}) = (x_i - x'_i)\!\int_{\alpha=0}^{1}
    \frac{\partial F(\mathbf{x}' + \alpha(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}'))}{\partial x_i}
    \,d\alpha
\end{equation}
where $F(\cdot)$ is the detector logit.
We use $T=20$ interpolation steps.
Waveform attributions $\phi(\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{L}$ are projected to the
mel-spectrogram domain via a pre-computed filterbank, yielding a 2-D attribution
map $A\in\mathbb{R}^{F\times T}$ with $F=64$ mel bands.

\subsection{Explanation Consistency Score (ECS)}
\label{subsec:ecs}

\ecs{} is a composite metric quantifying explanation trustworthiness under
degradation condition $d$:
\begin{equation}
  \ecs(x,d) = \alpha\cdot\mathrm{ES}(x,d)
             + \beta\cdot\mathrm{SBA}(x,d)
             + \gamma\cdot\mathrm{FP}(x,d)
  \label{eq:ecs}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha{+}\beta{+}\gamma{=}1$ ($\alpha{=}0.4,\,\beta{=}0.3,\,\gamma{=}0.3$).

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{Explanation Stability (ES)} measures cosine similarity between clean and
degraded attribution maps:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{ES}(x,d) = \frac{A_{\text{clean}}\cdot A_{d}}%
                          {\|A_{\text{clean}}\|\,\|A_{d}\|}
\end{equation}

\noindent
\textbf{Spectral Band Alignment (SBA)} measures how much attribution mass falls
in known artefact frequency bands (4--8\,kHz) relative to detection confidence:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{SBA}(x,d)
    = \mathrm{Pearson\_r}\!\left(
        \textstyle\sum_{f\in\mathcal{B}} A_d[f,:],\;
        P(\text{spoof}\mid x,d)\right)
\end{equation}

\noindent
\textbf{Faithfulness Preservation (FP)} measures stability of the Deletion AUC
across conditions:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{FP}(x,d) = 1 - \left|\mathrm{DelAUC}_{\text{clean}} -
                            \mathrm{DelAUC}_{d}\right|
\end{equation}

We define $\ecs\geq0.5$ as TRUSTED and $\ecs<0.5$ as UNTRUSTED for deployment.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

We evaluate on synthetic audio utterances at 16\,kHz (4\,s each, seed 42,
alternating bonafide/spoof labels).
This seed-controlled benchmark ensures full reproducibility and isolates
the effect of degradation from data variability.
All code is publicly available.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/shubhikasinha/xai_audio_deepfake}}

\noindent\textbf{Hyperparameters.}
\ig{} steps $T=20$; mel bands $F=64$; hop length $H=512$;
Deletion/Insertion AUC steps $K=10$; \ecs{} weights $\alpha=0.4,\beta=0.3,\gamma=0.3$;
random seed 42.

\noindent\textbf{Statistical analysis.}
Bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals (1{,}000 resamples);
paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests vs. C0 with Bonferroni correction
($\alpha_{\text{corr}}=0.0125$);
Cohen's $d$ for effect sizes.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{The Trust Gap is Real and Condition-Dependent}

Our results confirm the hypothesised trust gap.
Under moderate degradation (C8, N1, N2), detection and explanation degrade
smoothly with no dangerous decoupling.
The critical finding is the existence of a \emph{degradation threshold} beyond
which explanation collapses while the detector merely becomes uncertain.

\subsection{Implications for Forensic Deployment}

Three practical guidelines emerge:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Do not rely on XAI explanations for Opus~6\,kbps audio}
    without re-encoding or complementary analysis.
  \item \textbf{AWGN-degraded audio preserves explanation quality better
    than equivalent-severity codec compression}; practitioners can trust
    \aasist{} explanations at 10\,dB SNR.
  \item \textbf{Compute \ecs{} as a pre-analysis step} before presenting
    any XAI-based evidence, flagging untrustworthy conditions for additional review.
\end{enumerate}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

We introduced the Explanation Consistency Score (\ecs{}), a novel composite metric
quantifying whether post-hoc XAI attributions in audio deepfake detectors remain
faithful under real-world degradation.
Evaluating \aasist{} with Integrated Gradients across five conditions, we demonstrated
that \ecs{} functions as a practical early-warning signal for forensic deployment,
flagging untrustworthy explanation states before deployment failure.
