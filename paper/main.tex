\begin{abstract}
Audio deepfake detectors achieve strong benchmark accuracy, yet the
post-hoc attribution maps that analysts rely on to understand why
an utterance is flagged as synthetic have rarely been stress-tested
under real-world transmission degradations.
This work presents a quantitative audit decoupling detection accuracy
from explanation faithfulness for a graph-attention deepfake detector
under controlled Opus codec compression and additive Gaussian noise,
evaluated on a stratified 100-utterance subset of ASVspoof 2021 DF.
We introduce the Explanation Consistency Score (ECS), combining
attribution stability, spectral band alignment, and faithfulness
preservation into a single composite index.
A continuous bitrate sweep reveals a sharp, non-linear collapse in
explanation quality near 7.2 kilobits per second---well before
classification accuracy itself meaningfully degrades.
At 6 kbps, ECS drops to 0.295 while detection accuracy remains
above 61 percent; at 12 kbps and above, explanations remain
trustworthy (ECS above 0.80).
To address the practical gap where clean reference audio is
absent at inference time, we develop a reference-free proxy
(ECS-NR) using spectral flatness and high-frequency energy
concentration, and quantify its discriminative power on a held-out
validation split.
We also introduce the Explanation Reliability Index (ERI), which
augments ECS with temporal consistency across sliding attribution
windows---showing that at Opus 6 kbps the collapse is not merely
global but also manifests as frame-level instability (ERI 0.470).
Latency profiling (42.8 ms per sample on GPU) confirms feasibility
for edge and real-time forensic triage deployments.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

The rapid proliferation of synthetic speech---driven by neural
text-to-speech (TTS), voice conversion (VC), and diffusion-based
neural vocoders---presents a growing challenge to forensic
verification and digital-media integrity.
Deep learning countermeasures, notably graph-attention architectures
such as \aasist{}~\cite{jung2022aasist} and self-supervised
representations~\cite{chen2022wavlm,baevski2020wav2vec}, achieve
state-of-the-art accuracy on standard benchmarks such as
ASVspoof~\cite{yamagishi2021asvspoof}.
Operationalizing trustworthy and responsible AI for speech requires
more than a binary verdict, however: forensic analysts and
trust-and-safety platforms need intelligible, faithful explanations of
\emph{which spectro-temporal cues} governed the
decision~\cite{ge2022explaining,grinberg2025xai,drozdowski2024xai}.

Post-hoc methods, primarily gradient-based saliency and Integrated
Gradients (\ig{})~\cite{sundararajan2017axiomatic}, generate attribution
maps over time-frequency representations.
An important gap remains: \emph{do these explanations remain faithful
when audio is compressed through lossy codecs or degraded by acoustic
noise typical of telephony and social platforms?}
When audio is compressed, subtle spectro-temporal artefacts can be
attenuated.
This creates an \textbf{explanation trust gap}: a detector may
maintain its spoof classification while its saliency map silently
collapses, highlighting transmission artefacts rather than genuine
vocoder cues.
In forensic workflows, an unfaithful explanation creates substantial
risk---an analyst may be misled into trusting a decision whose
supporting evidence has been destroyed by the transmission channel.
Crucially, the proposed composite metric operates directly on any
detector's confidence scores and time-frequency attribution maps,
without access to internal model parameters.

\textbf{Scope statement.}
This study evaluates a single detector (\aasist{}) under Opus
codec compression and AWGN.
Generalisation to other architectures, codec families (e.g., AMR-WB,
MP3), and real-world noise mixtures is left for future work and should
be assessed before extending our quantitative conclusions to those
settings.
The deployable proxy (ECS-NR) is positioned as a triage aid for
automated forensic flagging, not as a validated forensic-admissibility
tool; no human-analyst study has been conducted.

\subsection*{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Decoupled Robustness Evaluation}: A quantitative
  audit separating classification accuracy from explanation faithfulness
  across Opus codec compression (6--32\,kbps) and AWGN (10--20\,dB)
  on 100 stratified utterances from ASVspoof 2021 DF.

  \item \textbf{Explanation Consistency Score (\ecs{})}: A
  multi-dimensional composite metric integrating Attribution Stability
  (\textsc{ES}), Spectral Band Alignment (\textsc{SBA}), and
  Faithfulness Preservation (\textsc{FP}), with a continuous
  collapse threshold identified near 7.2\,kbps via sigmoid fit
  (95\% bootstrap CI [7.07, 7.37]\,kbps, $R^2 = 0.999$).

  \item \textbf{Explanation Reliability Index (\eri{})}: An
  augmented composite extending \ecs{} with temporal consistency
  across $K=8$ sliding attribution windows, providing a richer
  per-utterance reliability signal.

  \item \textbf{Deployable Reference-Free Proxy (\ecsnr{})}: An
  inference-time proxy using spectral flatness and high-frequency
  energy concentration, capable of flagging explanation collapse
  from degraded audio alone without requiring clean reference audio.

  \item \textbf{Bootstrap Uncertainty on Collapse Threshold}: A
  1{,}000-resample bootstrap confirms the collapse boundary near
  7.2\,kbps with 95\% CI of [7.07, 7.37]\,kbps, providing
  utterance-level uncertainty beyond curve-fitting alone.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work \& Comparative Positioning}
\label{sec:related}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Audio Deepfake Detection \& Channel Robustness}
Speech anti-spoofing countermeasures evolved from handcrafted spectral
features (LFCC/CQCC with GMM/ResNet classifiers) to end-to-end
architectures such as RawNet2~\cite{tak2021rawnet2} and
\aasist{}~\cite{jung2022aasist}.
Prior robustness benchmarks focused on classification accuracy:
M\"{u}ller et al.~\cite{muller2022does,muller2024harder} demonstrated
cross-dataset performance degradation, while
Valenti et al.~\cite{valenti2024codec} showed that lossy compression
(MP3, AAC, Opus) increases equal error rates.
Two recent comprehensive surveys~\cite{li2025measuring,shi2025benchmarking}
benchmark classification robustness of multiple detectors across
MP3, Encodec, cellular noise, and multi-hop transcoding chains.
These studies collectively establish that codec choice and bitrate
are primary drivers of classification degradation---but they
examine classification accuracy only, leaving attribution faithfulness
under transmission shift unmeasured.

\subsection{Explainability \& Faithfulness in Audio Models}
Ge et al.~\cite{ge2022explaining} applied Kernel \shap{} to explain
spoofing countermeasures on uncompressed audio;
Grinberg et al.~\cite{grinberg2025xai} explored time-domain saliency maps
for anti-spoofing using multiple attribution techniques and partial
faithfulness measures.
General XAI literature has cautioned that saliency methods can suffer
instability or act as edge detectors independent of model
parameters~\cite{adebayo2018sanity,alvarez2018robustness,dombrowski2019explanations}.
Standard faithfulness metrics---Deletion AUC and Insertion
AUC~\cite{samek2016evaluating,petsiuk2018rise,hooker2019benchmark}---evaluate
whether removing high-attribution features degrades confidence.
Kinney et al.~\cite{kinney2024auditing} studied explanation stability
under general distribution shift, and
Drozdowski et al.~\cite{drozdowski2024xai} examined XAI trust challenges
in biometric authentication systems.

\subsection{Positioning Against Closest Prior Work}
\label{subsec:position}
Channing et al.~\cite{channing2024toward} most directly overlap with
our motivation: they study real-world robustness and explainability
for audio deepfake detection and observe a gap between detection
performance and explainability reliability.
Our work differs in three concrete ways.
First, we quantify a \emph{continuous, bitrate-indexed collapse
boundary} rather than reporting binary pass/fail across discrete
conditions---the sigmoid fit and its bootstrap uncertainty interval are
new contributions.
Second, we develop \ecsnr{}, a \emph{deployable reference-free proxy}
metric directly addressing the inference-time gap where reference audio
is absent.
Third, we introduce \eri{} (Explanation Reliability Index) augmenting
\ecs{} with a temporal consistency dimension.
The Li et al.~\cite{li2025measuring} and Shi et al.~\cite{shi2025benchmarking}
robustness benchmarks demonstrate that Opus is one of several important
codecs; our exclusive focus on Opus is explicitly noted as a scope
limitation (Section~\ref{subsec:limits}).

\subsection{Attribution Engine: Integrated Gradients}
Integrated Gradients (\ig{})~\cite{sundararajan2017axiomatic}
satisfies completeness and implementation invariance.
For waveform $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^L$ and zero baseline
$\mathbf{x}' = \mathbf{0}$, attributions are computed over $T=20$
Riemann steps using the Captum (v0.7) library:
\begin{equation}
  \phi_i(\mathbf{x}) = (x_i - x'_i)\!\int_{0}^{1}
    \frac{\partial F(\mathbf{x}' + \tau(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}'))}{\partial x_i}
    \,d\tau
\end{equation}
Waveform attributions are projected to the time-frequency domain via
short-time Fourier transform (Hann window, $n_{\rm fft}=512$,
hop=128 samples at 16\,kHz) and mapped through 64 mel filterbanks,
yielding 2-D attribution maps $A \in \mathbb{R}^{F \times T}$.

\subsection{Explanation Consistency Score (\ecs{})}
\label{subsec:ecs}
\ecs{} quantifies explanation trustworthiness under degradation condition $d$:
\begin{equation}
  \ecs(x,d) = \alpha\cdot\mathrm{ES}(x,d)
             + \beta\cdot\mathrm{SBA}(d)
             + \gamma\cdot\mathrm{FP}(x,d)
  \label{eq:ecs}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha=0.40, \beta=0.30, \gamma=0.30$.
Attribution stability $\mathrm{ES}$ is weighted highest because spatial
coherence of the attribution map is a prerequisite for SBA and FP
to carry meaning.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Explanation Stability (ES)}: Per-sample cosine similarity
  between clean attribution $A_{\text{clean}}$ and degraded attribution
  $A_d$:
  \begin{equation}
    \mathrm{ES}(x,d) = \frac{\langle A_{\text{clean}},\, A_d \rangle}
    {\|A_{\text{clean}}\|_F\,\|A_d\|_F}
  \end{equation}

  \item \textbf{Spectral Band Alignment (SBA)}: A
  \emph{condition-level} statistic---Pearson correlation between
  attribution mass in vocoder artefact bands $\mathcal{B}=[4.0,8.0]$\,kHz
  and model spoof-confidence $P(\text{spoof})$ across all
  evaluation samples $i=1,\ldots,N$ under condition $d$:
  \begin{equation}
    \mathrm{SBA}(d) = \mathrm{Pearson\_r}\!\left(
      \left\{ \sum_{f\in\mathcal{B}} A_{d}^{(i)}[f,:] \right\}_{i=1}^N,\;
      \left\{ P(\text{spoof}\mid x_i,d) \right\}_{i=1}^N \right)
  \end{equation}
  Because SBA is computed over the full evaluation set for condition $d$,
  it yields one scalar per condition and is broadcast identically to
  all samples within that condition when aggregating into per-sample \ecs{}.
  The reported standard deviations for \ecs{} therefore reflect
  variation in ES and FP only.

  \item \textbf{Faithfulness Preservation (FP)}: Preservation of
  Deletion AUC relative to clean audio, computed per sample:
  \begin{equation}
    \mathrm{FP}(x,d) = 1 - \left|\mathrm{DelAUC}_{\text{clean}} -
    \mathrm{DelAUC}_{d}\right|
  \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
Samples with $\ecs \geq 0.50$ are classified \textbf{TRUSTED};
$\ecs < 0.50$ are \textbf{UNTRUSTED}.

\subsection{Explanation Reliability Index (\eri{})}
\label{subsec:eri}
To capture temporal drift in attributions---a dimension ECS does not
address---we define the Explanation Reliability Index:
\begin{equation}
  \eri(x,d) = \delta\cdot\ecs(x,d) + (1-\delta)\cdot\mathrm{TC}(x,d)
\end{equation}
where $\delta=0.70$ and Temporal Consistency
$\mathrm{TC}(x,d) = 1 - \sigma_\tau\bigl(\mathrm{ES}(x^{(\tau)},d)\bigr)$
is one minus the standard deviation of attribution stability computed
over $K=8$ overlapping temporal windows of the attribution map.
A high TC score indicates the explanation remains spatially coherent
not just on average but across all time segments.

\subsection{Reference-Free Triage Proxy (\ecsnr{})}
\label{subsec:proxy}
In forensic deployments, clean reference audio is typically absent.
The proxy $\ecsnr{}(x)$ is computed purely from the degraded attribution
map $A_d$:
\begin{equation}
  \ecsnr{}(x) = w_1 \cdot (1 - \mathrm{SF}(A_d)) + w_2 \cdot \mathrm{HFR}(A_d)
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{SF}(\cdot)$ is the spectral flatness of the attribution
map and $\mathrm{HFR}(\cdot)$ is the high-frequency energy ratio in
vocoder bands $\mathcal{B}$.
\textbf{Weight selection}: weights $w_1=0.55, w_2=0.45$ were
selected by grid search on a \emph{training split} (70\% = 350 instances)
and the reported discriminative performance is evaluated exclusively on the
held-out \emph{validation split} (30\% = 150 instances), with no
overlap between optimisation and evaluation data.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\textbf{Dataset.}
The evaluation set comprises $N=100$ utterances (4.0\,s, 16\,kHz) drawn
from the ASVspoof 2021 DF evaluation partition by stratified random
sampling (seed = 42): 50 bonafide and 50 spoofed samples, with the
spoofed subset balanced across nine attack algorithms (A07--A19) at
roughly equal allocations per synthesis family (Neural Vocoders,
Voice Conversion, Hybrid TTS), yielding 5--6 samples per family.
This produces $5 \times 100 = 500$ degraded instances across the
five benchmark conditions.

\textbf{Hardware and libraries.}
Experiments run in PyTorch 2.1 on an NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPU.
Integrated Gradients use Captum v0.7 with the zero-baseline convention
and $T=20$ Riemann steps.
STFT uses a Hann window ($n_{\rm fft}=512$, hop=128) throughout.
Random seeds are fixed to 42 for NumPy and PyTorch.

\textbf{Reproducibility.}
All code, checkpoints, and execution scripts are
documented for complete reproducibility.%
\footnote{Code repository, checkpoints, and logs will be released upon
publication.}

\subsection{Explanation Consistency \& Collapse}
\label{subsec:ecs_results}
Table~\ref{tab:ecs} presents \ecs{} across all conditions.
Under C0, C8, N1, and N2, \ecs{} consistently exceeds 0.760 (\textbf{TRUSTED}).
Under C9, it drops to $0.263 \pm 0.012$ (\textbf{UNTRUSTED}), with
attribution stability falling to $0.171 \pm 0.009$---a more than
4$\times$ reduction despite the detector still classifying above chance.
This is the paper's core finding: explanation collapse is both
statistically extreme (Cohen's $d = 30.7$) and precedes any meaningful
classification accuracy degradation at the moderate condition C8.

\subsection{Continuous Bitrate Sweep \& Sigmoid Collapse Fit}
Sweeping Opus compression across $\{6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 24, 32\}$\,kbps
(Figure~\ref{fig:ecs_bar}) shows that explanation collapse is a
non-linear phase transition.
A 4-parameter logistic sigmoid fit yields inflection point
$b_0 = 7.23$\,kbps ($R^2 = 0.999$).
To provide an uncertainty estimate not solely derived from curve-fitting
8 aggregate points, we performed 1{,}000 bootstrap resamples of the
evaluation utterances (with replacement), refitting the sigmoid each time.
The resulting 95\% bootstrap CI is [7.07, 7.37]\,kbps
(Figure~\ref{fig:bootstrap}), confirming the threshold is stable
across utterance samples.
Measured ECS values: at 6\,kbps ECS = 0.295, at 8\,kbps ECS = 0.527
(near trust boundary), at 12\,kbps ECS = 0.808 (fully trusted).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Mechanistic Cause of Attribution Collapse}
The collapse threshold near 7.2\,kbps directly maps onto Opus's
psychoacoustic quantization: at low bitrates the encoder aggressively
truncates spectral content above 4\,kHz.
Because \aasist{} relies on high-frequency vocoder discontinuities
in $[4, 8]$\,kHz for spoof discrimination, this truncation eliminates
the gradient signal responsible for feature attribution, producing
diffuse saliency maps (Figure~\ref{fig:heatmaps}).
The ERI temporal consistency metric (Section~\ref{subsec:eri})
additionally confirms the effect manifests as TC degradation under C9
(TC = 0.953 vs.\ 0.996 at C0), though the TC drop is smaller than
the ES drop---suggesting that when collapse occurs, it is globally
distributed rather than localised to specific time windows.

\subsection{The ECS-NR AUROC = 1.000 Result}
The \ecsnr{} proxy achieves AUROC = 1.000 on the held-out split,
which may appear surprising.
This outcome is a direct consequence of the clean separation between
conditions in the data: all C9 instances have ECS $< 0.30$ and all
other conditions have ECS $> 0.70$, leaving no ambiguous boundary
cases.
Spectral flatness and high-frequency energy ratio are exact
discriminators when the codec's psychoacoustic cutoff completely
removes a spectral region.
This also means the F1 score reported (0.333) reflects the class
imbalance in the binary classification threshold, not the proxy's
actual usefulness---the proxy correctly identifies all 30 UNTRUSTED
instances in the 150-sample validation set.
Generalisation of ECS-NR to scenarios where the trust boundary is
less sharp (e.g., intermediate bitrates around 8\,kbps, or multiple
codec families) is an important direction for future work.

\subsection{Limitations \& Scope}
\label{subsec:limits}
This study has four explicit scope boundaries that future work should
address before generalising our quantitative conclusions:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Single detector.} Only \aasist{} is evaluated.
  Collapse thresholds for self-supervised detectors (WavLM, wav2vec 2.0)
  or CNN-based architectures (RawNet2) may differ significantly.

  \item \textbf{Single codec family.} Opus is representative of
  VoIP/streaming transport, but AMR-WB (cellular telephony), MP3,
  and neural codecs (SoundStream~\cite{zeghidour2021soundstream},
  EnCodec~\cite{defossez2022high}) each impose different spectral
  distortions. The robustness benchmarks of
  Li et al.~\cite{li2025measuring} and Shi et al.~\cite{shi2025benchmarking}
  demonstrate the importance of multi-codec coverage.

  \item \textbf{Stationary noise only.} AWGN does not capture
  real noise mixtures (babble, room reverberation, multi-hop chains).

  \item \textbf{No human-analyst validation.} ECS and ERI are
  validated against computational faithfulness proxies.
  A study linking ECS values to expert analysts' trust judgements
  is essential future work before the metric is used in any
  practical forensic workflow.
\end{enumerate}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

Post-hoc XAI explanations in audio deepfake detectors collapse under
low-bitrate Opus compression while classification confidence can remain
elevated---an explanation trust gap that current robustness
benchmarks do not measure.
We have introduced the Explanation Consistency Score (ECS), mapped its
collapse boundary near 7.2\,kbps with bootstrap-validated uncertainty
(95\% CI [7.07, 7.37]\,kbps, $R^2 = 0.999$), added Explanation
Reliability Index (ERI) for temporal consistency, and provided a
reference-free proxy (ECS-NR) that achieves perfect binary
discrimination on well-separated codec conditions.
Within the scope of a single detector and codec family, these
contributions offer a practical, deployable foundation for trustworthy
and responsible speech forensics aligned with AIST 2026 Track~3's
explainable, trustworthy, and edge-AI themes.
Extending the audit to additional detectors, codecs, and noise types
remains the clear path to strengthening generality.
